Design Statement

What I took away most from Everybody is that death is a part of life and can come for anyone at any time. To express this idea, I wanted to fully immerse the audience in the play by removing the traditional separation between stage and audience. W97’s flexible black box allows me to arrange the audience within the set, making them part of Everybody’s environment and bringing the play’s ideas about mortality into their own space.

I designed a space that feels familiar and almost like home, with windows, carpeting, and beds. Lying in a bed should offer comfort, while the surrounding environment makes the audience feel out of place. I chose red carpeting because people associated it with The Shining and its unsettling hotel setting. The neatly arranged beds also suggest a creepy orphanage, turning a place of rest into something institutional. The contrast between the bright red carpet and pale green floral wallpaper adds to that unease.

I considered placing artificial scenery outside the windows, but decided that leaving the view uncertain would better support the unsettling atmosphere. As the primary light source, the windows suggest a world outside without revealing what exists beyond the room.

During the grave scene, Everybody’s bed moves aside to reveal a grave that they climb into. Light and smoke transform this familiar space into something threatening, revealing that the comfort of the bed was only temporary.

The audience’s beds also conceal trapdoor graves. During La Danse Macabre, skeletons climb out from beneath them, bringing death directly into the audience’s personal space. This moment reinforces the central idea behind my design: the audience is no different from Everybody, and no one is exempt from death.
